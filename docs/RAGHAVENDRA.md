# Sri Raghavendra Tirtha — a study

Compiled 2026-11-15, before the acharya page is written, the way
`docs/SHANKARA.md` was. Every claim below is attributed. Where the
tradition and the record disagree, both are given.

---

## 1. Why the difficulty is the opposite of Shankara's

Shankara's life is known from hagiographies written five centuries
after him. Raghavendra's is close to the record:

- **A contemporary biography.** The *Raghavendra Vijaya* of
  Narayanacharya is described as contemporaneous (Wikipedia, citing
  Sharma). It is still a hagiography and reads as one.
- **Grants and dates.** A grant from Dodda Devaraya Odeyar of Mysore
  is dated to 1663. The year he entered the brindavana, 1671, is not
  disputed anywhere.
- **His own works.** About forty-five Sanskrit works are attributed to
  him and their attribution is not contested the way Shankara's is.

So the page does not need Shankara's three-tier warning about
authorship. It needs a different discipline: **separating the
historical life from the miracle literature that has grown around
it**, which is very large, devotional, and the main reason most
people know his name.

## 3. Family and early life

- A Kannada-speaking Madhva family of the Gautama gotra, "known for
  musicians and scholars" (Wikipedia).
- Great-grandfather Krishna Bhatta: tutor to Krishnadevaraya, by the
  family's tradition.
- Father Thimmanna Bhatta, mother Gopikamba; siblings Gururaja and
  Venkatamba. After Vijayanagara fell, the family moved to Kanchi.
- After his father's early death, his brother-in-law Lakshmi
  Narasimhacharya at Madurai saw to his education.
- He married (his wife is named Sarasvati in the tradition) and had a
  son, Lakshminarayanacharya.

## 4. Sannyasa and the matha

- Debates at Thanjavur drew the attention of **Sudhindra Tirtha**,
  pontiff of the matha at Kumbakonam. Venkatanatha was reluctant;
  he took sannyasa in 1621 (*Raghavendra Vijaya*).
- The matha was founded in the 15th century by **Vibudhendra Tirtha**
  at Kumbakonam and has been called the Kumbakonam, Dakshinadi and
  Vijayendra matha; it is now the **Sri Raghavendra Swamy Matha**,
  headquartered at Mantralaya. With the Uttaradi and Vyasaraja
  mathas it is one of the *mathatraya*, the three principal mathas in
  Jayatirtha's line (Wikipedia, Raghavendra Math).
- The matha's own list puts him seventeenth from Madhva:
  Madhva, Padmanabha, Narahari, Madhava, Akshobhya, Jayatirtha,
  Vidyadhiraja, Kavindra, Vagisha, Ramachandra, Vibudhendra,
  Jitamitra, Raghunandana, Surendra, Vijayindra, Sudhindra,
  Raghavendra. His successor was Yogindra Tirtha. The present pontiff
  is Subudhendra Tirtha (srsmatha.org; Wikipedia).
- **The tradition of earlier births**: Madhva tradition holds him to
  be Prahlada, and before him Shankukarna, and after Prahlada Bahlika
  and Vyasatirtha. This is printed as the tradition's belief, never as
  biography.

## 5. Travels

From Wikipedia, following the *Raghavendra Vijaya*: Kumbakonam;
south to Rameswaram, Ramnad, Srirangam and Madurai; west to Udupi and
Subrahmanya; north to Pandharpur, Kolhapur and Bijapur, where he "is
said to have" defeated Advaitins in debate (Wikipedia's own hedge,
kept); back to Kumbakonam; Mysore by 1663; then Mantralaya.

## 6. Mantralaya

- A village on the Tungabhadra, Kurnool district, Andhra Pradesh,
  then called **Manchale**.
- **The grant.** Devotional accounts say Siddi Masud, the Adil Shahi
  general who held Adoni, gave him the village, after a test in which
  food set before him turned to fruit and flowers. No primary record
  of the grant was found. **Decision:** the page says the matha's
  tradition credits Siddi Masud with the grant, and does not repeat
  the miracle as fact.
- **Why Manchale.** Tradition says Prahlada performed a yajna there in
  an earlier age. Printed as tradition.
- **The brindavana.** He entered it in 1671 on Shravana krishna
  dvitiya. Devotees hold that he is present in it still. Printed as
  belief.
- **The Aradhana**: three days — Purva Aradhana (pratipada), Madhya
  Aradhana (dvitiya, the day itself) and Uttara Aradhana (tritiya).
  Lunar dates only; the Festivals rule holds here too.
- **The Munro story.** W. Francis, in the *Madras District Gazetteer:
  Bellary* (1916), recorded that Sir Thomas Munro, sent to resume the
  matha's endowment, saw and spoke with Raghavendra and let it stand.
  Wikipedia flags that direct documentation is missing. **Decision:**
  printed as a story a colonial gazetteer recorded, nothing more.

About forty-five works in Sanskrit (Wikipedia; Sharma). Sharma's
judgment, quoted on Wikipedia: compact, simple, able to explain the
abstruse concepts of Dvaita in understandable terms.

signature *Venugopala*; *Indu enage Govinda* is attributed to him
(Wikipedia; the song is in this site's bhajans). Printed as
attributed.

## 8. What he taught

Madhva's Tattvavada, which the page states in its own terms rather
than as a foil to Advaita:

- The world is real, and difference is real: the five differences
  (*pancabheda*) — between God and soul, God and matter, soul and
  soul, soul and matter, matter and matter.
- Vishnu (Hari) is supreme and independent (*svatantra*); everything
  else is dependent (*paratantra*).
- Souls are graded (*taratamya*) and remain distinct in liberation.
- Liberation comes by the grace of Vishnu, earned through bhakti
  grounded in knowledge.

## 9. His following

Hebbar (cited on Wikipedia): his charisma and the miracles attributed
to him give him a following "that cuts across castes, sects and
creeds". Vijaya Dasa, Gopala Dasa and Jagannatha Dasa wrote songs to
him; this site's bhajans carry a group of them. Thursday is kept as
his day by many devotees.

The Raghavendra Stotra of **Appannacharya** opens with the verse most
devotees know. Its first verse, checked against sanskritdocuments.org
and sumadhwaseva.com, which agree syllable for syllable:

> पूज्याय राघवेन्द्राय सत्यधर्मरताय च ।
> भजतां कल्पवृक्षाय नमतां कामधेनवे ॥
